Task: assess the deal state and recommend at most 3 next steps that move this deal out of technical validation, following the playbook.

What a good step is (the same rubric the steps are graded on):
- Grounded: it names a fact, person, outcome or open item from this call. It could not apply to any other deal.
- Targets the binding constraint: it removes what currently stops the customer's next internal decision. Rank by this: the first step removes the biggest blocker.
- Buyer-enabling where possible: the output helps a customer stakeholder do a buying task and can be forwarded without the rep (a brief for an absent decision maker, a complete review packet, a trade-off comparison, a phased plan).
- Owned and dated: a named owner (the AE or SC by name, or a named customer contact for customer actions) and a due date.
- Honest and bounded: only the customer's own numbers, gaps stated plainly, no roadmap dates, no guessed timelines, no unverified savings.
- Respects the customer's process: supports their stated process and pace; offers rather than presses. Never recommend checking in, chasing, or pushing a meeting the customer deferred.
- Beyond baseline hygiene: sending the recap, sending an invite, updating CRM or raising an internal flag never qualifies on its own (see the playbook). The recap is already drafted for the rep.
- Feasible within the window: the AE or SC can complete it within 10 business days with assets that exist or can be built. Substantial work (a packet, a brief, a plan) is fine. Nothing that depends on a product release or a customer decision.

Fewer than 3 steps is normal. Never pad.

Fields:
- deal_state is one of: ready_to_advance, open_technical_gap, missing_stakeholder, competitive_risk, waiting_on_customer, low_information. Name the state of the binding constraint: a product gap that gates the customer's process is open_technical_gap; an incumbent or competitor that is the customer's default alternative is competitive_risk, even if the customer also deferred; waiting_on_customer only when the binding constraint is the customer's own process or internal alignment and Apex owes nothing material; low_information when little was said, and then at most 2 small steps. deal_state_why is 25 words or fewer.
- Each step: action is one sentence a rep can act on. owner, due_by (an ISO date on a business day after the as-of date, or empty when no date makes sense), playbook_rule_id (one of the playbook rules, PB-01 to PB-16), release_card_id (or empty), action_type (send_recap, book_meeting, send_material, route_to_sc, reach_stakeholder, support_customer_process, flag_deal_desk, resolve_with_champion, other), why (25 words or fewer: which blocker this removes and why it matters now), and at least one evidence item from the transcript.
- When two customer people contradict each other, follow PB-15: same facts to both sides, do not pick one.

watch_items: one for each competitor mentioned (name it and the release card to use, if any), and for any other risk the AE should keep an eye on. Leave empty if none.

The MEDDPICC gaps listed in the context are fields still unknown after the demo. Use them only if closing one is the binding constraint.
