# Best practice for next steps after an enterprise technical validation demo

Deep research output, run by the project owner from `docs/research/DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md` on 2026-10-03. Stored verbatim. Sources were not re-verified from this repo's environment (the network blocks gartner.com and gong.io).

The best-supported next step after a technical validation (TV) demo is not another seller touchpoint. It is material that helps the customer's buying group decide without you in the room. Evidence for this comes from analyst surveys (Gartner) and large call datasets (Dixon and McKenna, Gong): most stalled deals are lost to internal disagreement and fear of a wrong choice, not to a competitor. The Apex playbook covers seller hygiene well but has nothing for 3 high-value moves: a mutual action plan (MAP) that tracks customer-owned milestones, explicit handling of indecision, and recaps written for the stakeholders who were absent.

### 1. Summary

- **Answer first:** in the 1 to 10 business days after a TV demo, the strongest next steps (1) remove the specific blocker to the customer's next internal decision, (2) give the champion forwardable material for each absent decision maker, and (3) turn open items into dated, owned commitments the customer can see. Strength: moderate. This is an inference from analyst surveys and vendor call data, not from controlled trials.
- **The main threat is "no decision", not a competitor.** Dixon and McKenna's analysis of more than 2.5 million recorded sales conversations found that 40% to 60% of deals are lost to customers who say they intend to buy but never act. Of those "no decision" losses, 56% came from indecision and 44% from a preference for the status quo. Read the 56% as a share of no-decision losses, not of all deals.
- **Buying groups are larger and more conflicted than the old "6 to 10" figure suggests.** Gartner's 2025 survey of 632 buyers describes buying groups of 5 to 16 people across as many as 4 functions. In that survey, 74% of buyer teams showed "unhealthy conflict", and groups that reached consensus were 2.5 times more likely to report a high-quality deal (associated with, not caused by).
- **Sense making beats volume.** Customers who saw their rep as a "sense maker" (helping them weigh trade-offs and reconcile conflicting information) completed high-quality, low-regret purchases 80% of the time, against 50% for "telling" reps and 30% for "giving" reps (Gartner survey of more than 1,000 B2B customers, reported 2019 and in the Harvard Business Review (HBR) 2022). Sending generic collateral is the "giving" pattern.
- **Speed and specificity of follow-up are associated with better outcomes, but the evidence is vendor data.** Gong reports that following up within 24 hours is associated with a 14% higher win rate and 11% shorter deal duration (103,600 deals). The popular "within 2 hours" claim has no primary source that we could find.
- **Mutual action plans are widely recommended, but the evidence that they win deals is weak.** Outreach's "26% higher win rate" comes from its own platform data with obvious selection bias, and Outreach's own blog ("5 ways to increase win rates with Outreach") gives a different number for the same Success Plans feature: "push up win rates by as much as 13%". Its MAP feature page shows both the 26% and the 13% claims.
- **Playbook miss 1: no mutual action plan or close plan.** Nothing in PB-01 to PB-10 asks the rep to make customer-owned steps (security review, legal, procurement, budget approval) visible, dated and owned by the customer.
- **Playbook miss 2: no rule for indecision.** PB-10 partly covers implementation fear, but no rule tells the rep to give a clear recommendation, narrow the options and take risk off the table when the buyer fears making the wrong choice. That is the JOLT method's core prescription.
- **Playbook miss 3: the recap is written for the people in the room.** PB-01 ("include only what was said") and PB-07 are sound on honesty. They do not require the recap to be forwardable to absent stakeholders, which Gartner's buyer enablement and group-relevance findings suggest matters most.
- **Weak or conflicting rules:** PB-02 (push for a dated meeting within 2 business days) conflicts with PB-09 (do not push a meeting the customer deferred) unless a deferral path is added. PB-06's 1-day deal desk flag is internal hygiene, not a customer-facing step.

### 3. Best practice rules

```
- id: BP-01
  rule: End the call by proposing one specific next step with a date and time, and send a calendar invite for it the same day.
  when_it_applies: Any TV call where the customer has not deferred and no dated next step exists.
  why: Specific time-and-date requests are associated with more meetings booked within 10 days than open-ended asks (37% against 32%), though this is vendor data.
  evidence_ids: [E10, E11]
  strength: weak
  maps_to_playbook: PB-02
```

```
- id: BP-02
  rule: When the customer defers, ask what they need to work through, send one asset that addresses exactly that, and offer a check-in date they can accept or change instead of pressing for a meeting.
  when_it_applies: The customer says "let us digest this", "we'll be in touch", or declines a proposed meeting.
  why: Indecision drives most no-decision losses, and in the JOLT dataset 73% of reps responded to indecision by restarting their pitch, a pattern that increased the likelihood of losing the deal in 84% of those interactions (Challenger Inc., 2024), so reducing their uncertainty beats chasing.
  evidence_ids: [E07, E08, E21]
  strength: weak
  maps_to_playbook: missing (resolves the conflict between PB-02 and PB-09)
```

```
- id: BP-03
  rule: Send the recap within 24 hours, structured so the champion can forward it unedited: the customer's outcome in their words, what was proven, open items with owners and dates, and the proposed next step.
  when_it_applies: Every TV call.
  why: Follow-up within 24 hours is associated with a 14% higher win rate in Gong data, and group-relevant, shareable information is associated with better deal outcomes in Gartner surveys.
  evidence_ids: [E12, E02, E05]
  strength: moderate
  maps_to_playbook: PB-01, PB-07 (strengthens both)
```

```
- id: BP-04
  rule: For each absent decision maker who matters, give the champion a short brief (1 page or less) that answers that person's likely question in terms of the buying group's shared goal.
  when_it_applies: The economic buyer (EB), a security reviewer, a boss or a VP was absent or was mentioned as a future approver.
  why: Buying group relevance is associated with 3 times the likelihood of a high-quality deal, and buyers spend only 17% of buying time with suppliers, so most persuasion happens without the rep.
  evidence_ids: [E01, E02, E05, E06, E16]
  strength: moderate
  maps_to_playbook: PB-05
```

```
- id: BP-05
  rule: Before any commercial discussion, put the economic buyer in front of material framed around her outcome, risk and time to value, using the before and after scenario structure.
  when_it_applies: The EB is identified but has not engaged, or an EB meeting is booked.
  why: Won deals involve more buyer contacts (vendor data), and value-framing frameworks consistently recommend tying capabilities to the EB's stated outcomes.
  evidence_ids: [E09, E18, E23]
  strength: weak
  maps_to_playbook: PB-03
```

```
- id: BP-06
  rule: Once technical fit is shown and the EB is known, share a draft MAP that lists customer-owned milestones (security review, legal, procurement, budget approval) and ask the customer to correct the owners and dates.
  when_it_applies: After a successful TV, with an EB identified or about to be engaged.
  why: MAPs are associated with higher win rates in vendor data (26% in Outreach's data) and make the hidden customer-side process visible, but the evidence is selection-biased.
  evidence_ids: [E14, E15, E19, E07]
  strength: weak
  maps_to_playbook: missing
```

```
- id: BP-07
  rule: Confirm the technical win in writing against the success criteria agreed before the TV, naming any criteria that are still open.
  when_it_applies: A TV or POC has ended, or the customer says "the demo went well" without a formal technical decision.
  why: Practitioner guidance treats pre-agreed success criteria as the contract for awarding the technical win; without them, a good demo leaves no decision on record.
  evidence_ids: [E19, E08]
  strength: weak
  maps_to_playbook: missing
```

```
- id: BP-08
  rule: State any product gap plainly in writing, with the workaround and its cost to the customer, and never give a date for an unreleased feature.
  when_it_applies: A requirement is not met today (for example, nested single sign-on (SSO) claims with no general availability (GA) date).
  why: Buyers believe sense-making reps' claims far more than telling reps' claims (2/3 against 28%), and credibility is what keeps a gap from becoming a no-decision.
  evidence_ids: [E04, E03]
  strength: weak
  maps_to_playbook: PB-04, PB-08 (missing the "no roadmap dates" clause)
```

```
- id: BP-09
  rule: For customer-owned processes, deliver a complete packet before the customer's deadline, offer a named subject matter expert (SME) for questions, and follow up only when you have new information.
  when_it_applies: Security review, legal review or procurement is in progress or scheduled.
  why: Buyer enablement (information that helps buyers complete buying tasks) is associated with easier purchases and larger, lower-regret deals, while generic nudges are associated with fewer meetings.
  evidence_ids: [E05, E21, E03]
  strength: weak
  maps_to_playbook: PB-09
```

```
- id: BP-10
  rule: Quantify value only with numbers the customer stated, show every assumption, and present a range rather than a point estimate.
  when_it_applies: The customer stated an outcome (for example, "a week or more out of each screening campaign") or asks for return on investment (ROI).
  why: Overclaiming is the "telling" pattern associated with lower buyer belief, while value frameworks tie metrics to the customer's own PBOs.
  evidence_ids: [E04, E18, E03]
  strength: weak
  maps_to_playbook: PB-07 (partial)
```

```
- id: BP-11
  rule: When implementation effort or a past failed project comes up, name the past failure, then show a phased plan with named owners, customer hours by role, an early measurable milestone and explicit exit points.
  when_it_applies: Anyone mentions timing, effort, a past migration or a past project that ran long.
  why: "Take risk off the table" is one of the 4 JOLT behaviors for indecision, and implementation problems are a leading driver of software purchase regret.
  evidence_ids: [E08, E20, E07]
  strength: moderate
  maps_to_playbook: PB-10 (add naming the past failure, early milestone and exit points)
```

```
- id: BP-12
  rule: When a competitor or incumbent is named, give the champion an honest trade-off comparison on the customer's own workload, including where the incumbent is good enough.
  when_it_applies: A named competitor, or the question "why not extend what we already have?"
  why: Helping buyers reconcile conflicting options (sense making) is associated with 80% high-quality deals against 50% for telling, and Gong Labs data associates early competitive discussion with a 49% greater chance of closing than greenfield deals (Orlob, 2017).
  evidence_ids: [E03, E04, E22]
  strength: moderate
  maps_to_playbook: PB-06 (PB-06's deal desk flag is internal; this adds the customer-facing step)
```

```
- id: BP-13
  rule: When the buyer shows fear of a wrong choice, give a clear recommendation, narrow the options to 1 or 2, and offer a risk reducer such as a phased start or an exit criterion, instead of adding more information or fear, uncertainty and doubt (FUD).
  when_it_applies: Hedging language ("timing", "need to digest", "sort of"), requests for more options or references, or a stated past failure.
  why: Indecision (omission bias) accounts for 56% of no-decision losses in the JOLT dataset, and adding information is associated with buyers settling for smaller purchases.
  evidence_ids: [E07, E08, E03]
  strength: moderate
  maps_to_playbook: missing
```

```
- id: BP-14
  rule: After a low-information call, re-qualify by asking 1 direct question about the business outcome and decision process before committing more SC time, and mark the deal at risk in the forecast.
  when_it_applies: No outcome stated, key evaluator lukewarm or left early, no next meeting.
  why: Indecision and non-commitment can be seen early in calls (87% of calls show moderate or high indecision), and widening gaps between interactions predict no decision.
  evidence_ids: [E07, E08]
  strength: weak
  maps_to_playbook: PB-07 (partial)
```

```
- id: BP-15
  rule: When the buying group disagrees internally, give both sides the same neutral facts they need and offer an SME session with both present, rather than taking a side.
  when_it_applies: Two customer functions disagree (for example, IT and compliance on hosting region).
  why: 74% of buyer teams show unhealthy conflict, and groups that reach consensus are 2.5 times more likely to report a high-quality deal (Gartner, 2025).
  evidence_ids: [E02, E03]
  strength: moderate
  maps_to_playbook: missing
```

### 4. Rubric for judging a recommended next step

The 2 proposed criteria should be replaced. "Doable in under 10 minutes" penalizes the highest-value steps: a complete security packet or a phased implementation plan takes hours, and the evidence (E05, E11) favors substance over speed. Replace it with "feasible within the window" (R8). "A typical AE would not do it unprompted" cannot be observed by a grader and rewards novelty for its own sake. Replace it with "adds beyond baseline hygiene" (R7), defined against an explicit hygiene list.

| ID | Criterion | Pass if | Fail if | Passing example | Failing example | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Grounded in this call | The step names a fact, person, outcome or open item from the transcript | The step could apply to any deal | "Send the COO a time-to-value plan that leads with catching late loads while dispatch can still reroute" | "Send a value-focused follow-up" | E02, E18 |
| R2 | Targets the binding constraint | The step addresses what currently prevents the customer's next internal decision | The step improves something that is not blocking | Meridian: "Deliver the full security packet, including the written SSO design, before the Oct 9 committee" | Meridian: "Send a customer case study" | E07, E08 |
| R3 | Buyer-enabling | The output helps a customer stakeholder complete a buying task and can be forwarded without the rep | The output only serves the seller (internal flags, chasers) | "1-page brief for the champion's boss on extending Lakestream against adopting our product, on their workload" | "Flag Lakestream to deal desk" as the customer-facing step | E05, E06, E02 |
| R4 | Owned and dated | The step names an owner (AE, SC or a named customer role) and a date or deadline | The step has no owner or no timing | "SC sends the row-level access answer on Tuesday Oct 6 and AE confirms it in writing" | "Look into the partner hierarchy question" | E12, E11 |
| R5 | Honest and bounded | Claims are limited to the customer's numbers, gaps are stated, and no roadmap or guessed timelines appear | The step promises a GA date, a guessed go-live or unverified ROI | "State that nested claims have no GA date and lay out the token-change workaround with its 6 to 8 week review" | "Tell them nested claims are coming next quarter" | E04, E03 |
| R6 | Respects the customer's process | The step supports the customer's stated process and pace, or offers rather than presses | The step pushes a meeting the customer deferred or sends "checking in" messages | "Offer a check-in date the champion can accept or change" | "Call Monday to lock in a meeting" after "we'll be in touch" | E07, E21 |
| R7 | Adds beyond baseline hygiene | The step goes beyond the hygiene list (recap sent, invite sent, CRM updated, internal flag raised) | The step is only a hygiene item | "Coach the champion on how her boss will challenge the deal and give her the answer" | "Update the opportunity stage in CRM" | E16, E03 |
| R8 | Feasible within the window | The AE or SC can complete it in 1 to 10 business days with assets that exist or can be built | It depends on things outside the seller's control (product releases, customer decisions) | "Draft a phased onboarding plan with hours by role" | "Ship nested claims support before the committee" | E19, E20 |

A recommended step should pass R1, R2, R4, R5 and R6 to be acceptable. R3 and R7 separate strong steps from adequate ones.

### 5. Reference answers for the 5 scenarios

Dates assume today is Saturday Oct 3, 2026, so "Tuesday" is Oct 6.

```
- scenario: Halvorsen Freight
  must_have_steps:
    - step: SC delivers the row-level access design for the partner hierarchy by Tuesday Oct 6 as committed, and the AE confirms it in writing to the champion so it is closed before the COO session.
      rule: PB-04, PB-08
      why_first: An open technical item at the Oct 8 COO meeting would let the COO's time-to-value doubt attach to a concrete gap.
    - step: Build and send to the champion by Oct 7 a phased time-to-value plan for the COO that opens with the first measurable milestone for catching late loads, with named owners and customer hours by role, and explicitly contrasts it with an 8 month project.
      rule: BP-11, PB-10
      why_first: The COO is the economic buyer, and her known fear is a repeat of the 8 month project; taking that risk off the table is what the meeting will decide.
    - step: Agree the Oct 8 agenda with the champion and bring a draft MAP showing customer-owned steps (budget approval, security, procurement) for the COO to correct at the end of the session.
      rule: BP-06, BP-05
```

### 6. Open questions

- **Does post-TV follow-up speed cause better outcomes, or do better reps simply follow up faster?** All timing data (E12, E13) is vendor data from the vendor's own customers. Outcome data that would settle it: for each deal, hours from TV call to recap, and whether the deal advanced within 30 days, controlling for rep tenure and deal size.
- **Do MAPs help or only mark deals that are already winning?** E14 and E15 cannot separate the two. Test: compare win rates where a draft MAP was shared within 10 business days of TV against deals with no MAP, matched on stage and size, and record whether the customer edited the plan.
- **Which champion material moves absent stakeholders?** Gartner's group-relevance finding (E02) is survey-based. Track whether forwarded briefs (opened by new contacts) are associated with new stakeholders joining within 14 days.
- **How should a deferral be handled?** No primary study compares "respect the deferral and send an asset" against "propose a meeting anyway". Outcome data: response rate and advancement after each approach.
- **How well does JOLT replicate?** The 40% to 60% and 56% figures (E07) come from one dataset analyzed by authors who sell training. Independent replication is lacking.
- **Gartner's 2.8 times figure is unresolved.** Gartner's site confirms "3 times more likely to buy a bigger deal with less regret", but the 2.8 times purchase-ease wording and its sample size (250 or 750) could only be found in secondary sources.
- **Calibration target for Apex:** log every recommended step with type (from the BP list), days after TV, owner, and whether the customer responded. Then test which step types taken within 5 business days are associated with the deal reaching the next stage within 30 days.
