# Apex Enterprise TV playbook (fictional)

Synthetic data, fictional company. These rules are invented for this prototype. They describe what good looks like in the window after a technical validation (TV) demo. Each rule has a stable ID that next steps must cite.

## PB-01: recap within 24 hours
Send the customer a written recap within 24 hours of the TV demo. Include only what was said: what was validated, what is still open, who owns what, and by when.

## PB-02: dated next meeting
A dated next meeting is agreed with the customer in the call, or within 2 business days of it. "We will be in touch" is not a next step.

## PB-03: economic buyer by day 5
The economic buyer is named by day 5 of TV and has a planned touchpoint before any commercial discussion. If someone in the call names who signs, record it even if it was said in passing.

## PB-04: technical gaps routed to the SC with a date
Every unresolved technical item is routed to the SC with an owner and a due date the customer heard. If the answer depends on Apex product or engineering, the SC gets a dated answer, not a roadmap slide.

## PB-05: at least 2 customer contacts, and absent stakeholders get a path in
Keep at least 2 engaged customer contacts. When a stakeholder who matters to the decision was named but absent, give the champion something specific to bring to that person.

## PB-06: competitor mentions go to deal desk
Flag any named competitor to deal desk within 1 business day. Answer competitive questions on the customer's actual workload, not with general claims. Use a release card when one applies.

## PB-07: recap ties to the customer's outcome in their words
The recap connects what was shown to the business outcome the customer stated, in their own words. If no outcome was stated, say so internally and ask for it. Never invent one.

## PB-08: SC commitments are logged and closed
Any commitment the SC makes in the call is logged with an owner and a date, and the customer is told when it is done. Commitments said in passing count.

## PB-09: support customer-owned processes, do not chase
When progress depends on a customer-owned process (security review, legal, procurement, internal alignment), send what that process needs, sized to its deadline. Do not send "checking in" messages. Offer help; do not push for a meeting the customer has deferred.

## PB-10: implementation concerns get a real plan
When a customer raises implementation effort or team capacity, even politely, respond with a realistic onboarding plan with named owners and customer effort by role. Do not guess a timeline in the call.
