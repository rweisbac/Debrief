# Apex Enterprise TV playbook, v2 (fictional)

Synthetic data, fictional company. These rules are invented for this prototype. Version 2 is rebuilt from the best practice research in `docs/research/best_practice_findings.md`: rules PB-01 to PB-10 are kept and amended, and PB-11 to PB-16 add the moves the research found missing. Each rule names its evidence (E-numbers in the research's evidence table) and how strong that evidence is. None of the evidence is causal; most is analyst surveys or vendor data.

The goal in the window after a technical validation (TV) demo: help the customer's buying group decide without Apex in the room. Most stalled deals are lost to indecision and internal disagreement, not to a competitor (E07, E02).

## Baseline hygiene (not a next step on its own)

These are expected after every demo and never count as a recommended step by themselves: sending the recap, sending the calendar invite for an agreed meeting, updating CRM, and raising an internal flag (for example to deal desk). A step may include one of these only when it carries something beyond it.

## PB-01: recap within 24 hours, written to be forwarded
Send the customer a written recap within 24 hours. Include only what was said. Write it so the champion can forward it unedited to people who were not in the call: the customer's outcome in their words, what was proven, open items with owners and dates, and the proposed next step. Evidence: E12, E02, E05 (moderate).

## PB-02: propose one dated next step
When the customer has not deferred and no dated next step exists, propose one specific next step with a date and time, and send the invite the same day. "We will be in touch" is not a next step. If the customer defers, follow PB-11 instead; never press. Evidence: E11, E10 (weak).

## PB-03: economic buyer before commercials
The economic buyer is named by day 5 of TV. Before any commercial discussion, put the economic buyer in front of material framed around their outcome, risk and time to value, using a before and after picture. If someone names who signs in passing, record it. Evidence: E09, E18 (weak).

## PB-04: technical gaps stated plainly, with an owner and a date
Route every unresolved technical item to the SC with an owner and a date the customer heard. State any product gap plainly in writing, with the workaround and its cost to the customer. Never give a date for an unreleased feature; the SC gets a dated answer, not a roadmap slide. Evidence: E04, E03 (weak).

## PB-05: a brief for each absent decision maker
Keep at least 2 engaged customer contacts. For each absent decision maker who matters (economic buyer, security reviewer, a boss or VP), give the champion a short brief, 1 page or less, that answers that person's likely question in terms of the buying group's shared goal. Evidence: E01, E02, E05, E06, E16 (moderate).

## PB-06: competitors answered on the customer's workload
Flag a named competitor to deal desk within 1 business day (internal hygiene). The customer-facing step is an honest trade-off comparison on the customer's own workload, including where the competitor or incumbent is good enough, given to the champion. Never disparage. Use a release card when one applies. Evidence: E03, E04, E22 (moderate).

## PB-07: the customer's outcome, in their words and numbers
Tie the recap and every value statement to the business outcome the customer stated, in their words. Quantify value only with numbers the customer stated, show every assumption, and give a range, not a point estimate. If no outcome was stated, say so internally and ask for it. Never invent one. Evidence: E04, E18, E03 (weak).

## PB-08: SC commitments are logged and closed
Any commitment the SC makes in the call is logged with an owner and a date, and the customer is told when it is done. Commitments said in passing count. Evidence: E12 (weak to moderate).

## PB-09: support customer-owned processes, do not chase
When progress depends on a customer-owned process (security review, legal, procurement, internal alignment), deliver a complete packet before the customer's deadline, offer a named subject matter expert for questions, and follow up only when there is new information. No "checking in" messages. Evidence: E05, E21, E03 (weak).

## PB-10: implementation fear gets a phased plan
When anyone raises implementation effort, timing, team capacity, or a past project that ran long, name the past failure, then show a phased plan with named owners, customer hours by role, an early measurable milestone and explicit exit points. Do not guess a timeline. Evidence: E08, E20, E07 (moderate).

## PB-11: a deferral gets one useful asset, not a chase
When the customer defers ("let us digest this", "we'll be in touch", or declines a meeting), ask what they need to work through, send one asset that addresses exactly that, and offer a check-in date they can accept or change. Do not press for a meeting. Evidence: E07, E08, E21 (weak).

## PB-12: a draft mutual action plan once the economic buyer is known
After a successful TV, with the economic buyer identified or about to be engaged, share a draft mutual action plan that lists the customer-owned milestones (security review, legal, procurement, budget approval) and ask the customer to correct the owners and dates. Evidence: E14, E15, E19, E07 (weak; vendor data with selection bias).

## PB-13: confirm the technical win in writing
When a TV ends, or the customer says the demo went well without a formal technical decision, confirm the technical win in writing against the success criteria agreed before the TV, naming any criteria that are still open. Evidence: E19, E08 (weak).

## PB-14: indecision gets a recommendation, not more options
When the buyer shows fear of a wrong choice (hedging such as "timing", "need to digest", "sort of", requests for more options, or a stated past failure), give a clear recommendation, narrow the options to 1 or 2, and offer a risk reducer such as a phased start or an exit criterion. Do not add more information or fear, uncertainty and doubt. Evidence: E07, E08, E03 (moderate).

## PB-15: internal disagreement gets the same facts on both sides
When two customer functions disagree (for example IT and compliance), give both sides the same neutral facts they need and offer a session with an Apex subject matter expert with both present. Never take a side or give legal advice. Evidence: E02, E03 (moderate).

## PB-16: a low-information call is re-qualified
When no outcome was stated, the key evaluator was lukewarm or left early, and no next meeting exists, ask 1 direct question about the business outcome and the decision process before committing more SC time, and mark the deal at risk internally. Evidence: E07, E08 (weak).
